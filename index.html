06 · The finding

One block of questions broke in two.

Eleven statements asked about other species in Krafslösaskogen. Seven were about the forest itself — whether it shelters, supports and is open to all the life in it. Four were about decisions — whether the city’s choices recognise that life. They were written as one scale. They did not behave as one.

07 · How the pieces fit

A chain, and something outside it.

A path model links living close by, visiting often, feeling attached and reporting well-being. It fits the data well and accounts for most of the variance in well-being. Belief about institutional recognition sits largely outside that chain.

10 · Reading this carefully

What this can and cannot show.

The sample chose itself

Cross-sectional, so no causal claims

Everything was measured once, from the same person, in the same sitting. The path model’s arrows encode a theory, not a demonstration; the same covariances can fit rival orderings. Common-method variance may also inflate correlations among the attitude scales.

What was cleaned

Two responses containing explicit test text were removed. The three respondents aged under 18 are also excluded from every analysis and from the public map. Four world-wrapped longitudes were unwrapped to their equivalent longitude; one unrecoverable latitude was dropped. For the public map, a further 47 pins outside the checked story area are hidden so the spatial story stays with Krafslösaskogen, Snurrom, Björkenäs and Hörsö-Värsnäs.

How scales were built

The gap needs a follow-up, not a conclusion

Low institutional-recognition scores are consistent with several explanations: judgement about this specific planning process, more general trust in municipal decision-making, the timing of a live dispute, or unfamiliarity with what institutions assessed. This survey cannot separate them. What it does show is that the forest–institution gap is broad across the measured subgroups and only weakly related to the respondent characteristics available here.

Timing

Responses were collected between 22 April and 6 July 2026. The dataset is therefore a snapshot of one period, not a longitudinal measure of how views changed over time.

Statistics

Regression standard errors are heteroskedasticity-consistent (HC3). Correlation p-values are corrected for multiple comparisons using Benjamini–Hochberg. Indirect effects use 5,000 bootstrap resamples with percentile intervals. The path model is estimated on standardised observed composites, so its fit indices describe the structural relations among scales.

Reproducing it

All analytical values in the advanced sections are generated from the supplied analysis pipeline and rebuilt after excluding respondents under 18. The original questionnaire remains available through the link below.
